How long does a water purifier filter really last? Who decides?

A filter's life is measured in litres, not months. A CCTV News report from November 2026 shows what happens when a seller answers in months instead — and why the honest answer starts with two numbers: the rated volume of the filter, and the water quality where it will be used.

The case that started it

A consumer identified in the report as Mr. Wang bought a water dispenser for more than ¥3,200 after the product page highlighted the durability of its filters and stated clearly that a filter would last one year. About a week later, he looked at the machine's display and saw the filter-life indicator had already dropped a bar.

He is not alone. Reporters searched Heimao, a Chinese consumer-complaint platform, for "water purifier filter" and found more than 1,000 related complaints, concentrated on three things: a large gap between the claimed filter life and the actual service life, sellers hiding the preconditions behind their claims, and disputes over who is responsible after the sale.

The report asks the question every buyer should ask: when a filter needs changing, who decides — the seller, or the filter itself?

Months are the wrong unit

Most buyers judge a filter by months or years. That is exactly where vague claims hide, according to Yang Chao, director of the science communication centre at Shenzhen University of Technology and a PhD in analytical chemistry, interviewed in the report. Filters do different jobs, so their replacement cycles are different. As general guidance, he puts PP cotton filters at 3–6 months, activated carbon filters at 6–12 months, ultrafiltration membranes at 1–2 years, and RO membranes at 2–3 years.

But those ranges are guidance, not a specification. The scientific measure, Yang explains, is the rated total purified water volume — in litres, under standard test conditions. It is the total volume of water a filter can treat while the output still meets the standard. That is the compliant, verifiable basis for filter life. A seller who simply promises "one filter change a year" without saying which filter stage, or under what water conditions, is easy to believe — and easy to be misled by.

What actually decides how long a filter lasts

Three things decide it, and none of them is the calendar.

First, the inlet water. If the incoming water does not meet the purifier's requirements, filter life falls. In hard-water areas, calcium and magnesium ions form scale that clogs the membrane, so both filter life and water output drop. Second, the cumulative volume treated. Some households use a lot of water and some use little — the same filter reaches its rated volume months apart in two different homes. Third, water temperature, water pressure, and maintenance.

That is why a filter-life display can drop a bar in a week without anything being broken. The display tracks treated volume, not time. A heavy user on hard water will burn through the rated volume far faster than a light user on soft water — and a promise of "one year" was never a promise about either of them.

Poor water is not a free pass for sellers

Hard water shortens filter life. But it cannot be used to hide a defective product or an inflated claim. The report draws that line clearly: if a seller advertises a filter life of one year and the actual life is under a month, that can amount to false advertising — and possibly consumer fraud.

Huang Yinxu, associate professor at Renmin University Law School, explains that such a claim can breach China's Advertising Law, because the stated performance and validity period do not match reality and materially affect the purchase decision. It can also breach the Consumer Rights Protection Law, including the duty to provide truthful information. Where the conduct meets the standard for fraud, consumers may claim a refund plus three times compensation.

For a brand owner, the lesson is practical, not just legal. A filter claim your customers cannot verify becomes your after-sales problem. Complaints about filter life are complaints about your brand, not about the seller who wrote the product page.

The question to ask instead

Before you order, ask for two numbers — and ask them in writing.

The first is the rated total purified water volume of each filter stage, in litres, and the test conditions behind it. The second is your local water data: TDS and total hardness in your market. A supplier that can only answer "one year" is not giving you a number you can check. A supplier that answers in litres, and then adjusts for your water, is.

That is how we work. We quote rated volume, not months: Grade 1 4,000 litres, Grade 2 3,000 litres, Grade 3 2,000 litres. And before we state how long a filter will last, we check the local water quality in your market — TDS and total hardness first. In soft-water areas a filter can reach its full rated volume, sometimes more. In hard-water areas, where hardness above 200 ppm makes scaling likely, we discount the life rather than promise the full number. You get a filter life your customers can actually experience, and a replacement-filter business built on real consumption, not on a slogan.

When a supplier tells you how long a filter lasts, ask what the number is measured in. If the answer is months, ask again. If the answer is litres — and it comes with a question about your water — you are talking to a supplier who will still be standing behind the product a year later.

FAQ

How long does an RO membrane last?
As general guidance cited in the CCTV News report, an RO membrane is usually replaced every 2–3 years. The accurate answer for any specific product is its rated total purified water volume in litres: a membrane that has treated its rated volume is due for replacement, whether that takes one year or three.

Why did my filter-life display drop so fast?
The display tracks cumulative treated water volume, not calendar time. A household that uses a lot of water, or water with high hardness, reaches the filter's rated volume much sooner than a light user on soft water.

Does hard water really shorten filter life?
Yes. In hard-water areas, calcium and magnesium form scale that clogs the membrane, reducing both filter life and water output. That is why filter life should be confirmed against local water quality before it is promised.

What should I ask a supplier before ordering?
Ask for the rated total purified water volume of each filter stage in litres, the test conditions behind that number, and what happens to filter life in hard water. A supplier that can only answer in months is not giving you a verifiable number.

Do you quote filter life in months?
No. We quote rated total purified water volume — Grade 1 4,000 litres, Grade 2 3,000 litres, Grade 3 2,000 litres — and we check your local water quality, including TDS and total hardness, before stating how long a filter will last in your market.
