RO vs UF vs nanofiltration — which filtration fits your market?

24 November 2026

Reverse osmosis (RO) offers the finest separation and reduces dissolved salts and total dissolved solids (TDS). Ultrafiltration (UF) removes suspended solids and bacteria and reduces turbidity, while dissolved salts and hardness stay in the water. Nanofiltration (NF) sits between them, reducing hardness and some dissolved solids while retaining some minerals. Start with the water that will enter the purifier. What needs to be removed from that feed water determines which technology belongs in your product range.

What each technology removes

Reverse osmosis (RO)

RO provides the broadest treatment of the three. It reduces dissolved salts and TDS, including hardness minerals and many heavy metals, and an intact membrane can reject bacteria and viruses. It also lowers the levels of minerals a buyer might want to retain. RO needs pressure, and systems with a booster pump need electricity. The membrane splits the incoming water into treated water and a concentrated waste stream that needs a drain. Ask for recovery data if drain volume matters for your market.

Ultrafiltration (UF)

UF uses a porous membrane to capture suspended particles, reduce turbidity and retain bacteria. Some membranes also retain some viruses. Dissolved salts pass through, so TDS and dissolved hardness stay largely unchanged. UF operates at relatively low pressure and keeps dissolved minerals in the treated water. A typical point-of-use unit has no continuous wastewater stream, though designs that flush or backwash the membrane discharge water during cleaning.

Nanofiltration (NF)

NF is often used to soften water because it preferentially rejects calcium and magnesium ions. It also reduces sulfate, some dissolved organic compounds and colour. NF lowers TDS partially because some salts pass through, so the treated water retains some minerals. It generally runs at lower pressure than RO and can produce less wastewater at higher recovery. Ask how much hardness and TDS the proposed system leaves in the treated water under your feed conditions.

Match the technology to the water

High TDS or salty, brackish water
Start with RO when the treatment goal is to lower salinity. Check that the system is rated for the feed-water TDS you measured.

Treated municipal water with sediment or turbidity
Consider UF when the supply is already microbiologically treated and the main job is removing suspended material.

Hard water where some minerals should remain
Consider NF when you want to lower calcium and magnesium while retaining some dissolved minerals. Check the expected outlet hardness.

Very poor or unknown source water
Test it first. RO with suitable prefiltration is the conservative starting point for evaluating broad treatment. Confirm the full treatment train against the test results, including any need for disinfection, before relying on the output for drinking.

Measure both TDS and total hardness before choosing. TDS describes the overall amount of dissolved material, while hardness primarily reflects calcium and magnesium. If you sell into several areas, compare local feed-water data before specifying one system across the market. Confirm the choice against the inlet limits and tested performance for each model and order.

What to ask before you order

  • What is the membrane’s nominal pore size or molecular weight cut-off?
  • Which particles, microbes and dissolved substances does the system reduce, and which pass through?
  • What test data support those reductions and under what inlet conditions?
  • How will the local TDS and total hardness affect output, removal performance and maintenance?
  • Does installation require electricity or a drain? Is remineralisation available if the target water profile calls for it?

Confirm specifications per model and order.

FAQ

Which removes the most contaminants?
RO offers the broadest removal of the three, including dissolved salts, hardness and many heavy metals. Verify each removal claim for the specific system.

Does UF remove TDS or hardness?
No. The dissolved salts that make up TDS, including dissolved calcium and magnesium, generally pass through a UF membrane.

Is nanofiltration enough for salty water?
Usually not when desalination is the goal. NF lets some salts through. Start with RO and match its feed-water rating to the measured salinity.

Which one keeps minerals in the water?
UF retains dissolved minerals in the treated water. NF lets some minerals pass but reduces calcium and magnesium. RO reduces dissolved minerals broadly.

How do I choose for my market?
Test local feed water for TDS and total hardness, identify the treatment goal and confirm performance and installation requirements for the proposed model and order.
