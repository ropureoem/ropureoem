Countertop vs under-sink RO: which one fits your market?

If you are choosing your first RO product line, the first decision is not the brand or the colour. It is the form: a countertop machine that sits on the kitchen worktop, or an under-sink system that hides under the counter with only a faucet showing. The two forms sell to different buyers, need different installation, and deliver water at very different rates. This guide compares them so you can pick the form your market actually wants.

Installation: plug in vs plumb in

A countertop RO needs no plumbing work. You place it on the worktop, fill the raw-water tank, plug it in, and it produces purified water. That is the whole installation. It suits buyers who cannot or do not want to modify their kitchen — renters, people in small flats, offices that need a machine today, and markets where hiring a plumber for a water purifier is not normal.

An under-sink RO is plumbed into the household water supply and mounted under the kitchen sink, with a dedicated faucet on the sink. It needs under-sink space, a water connection and a drain connection, and someone to fit it. Once installed it is out of sight and out of the way, and it draws water directly from the mains — there is no tank to refill.

For a brand owner, the difference is also a sales difference. A countertop machine can be sold to anyone with a kitchen worktop and a power socket. An under-sink system is sold to homeowners and to buyers who are already renovating or replacing a kitchen — a smaller pool, but one that buys a more permanent product.

Water flow and capacity: what the numbers mean

The clearest technical gap between the two forms is flow rate.

Countertop RO units in our range run at 50–100 GPD, with RO production around 0.13–0.26 L/min. That is enough for drinking water and cooking for a small household, dispensed a glass at a time. A countertop machine with a raw-water tank produces water into its own pure-water tank, so the flow you feel at the tap depends on the tank as well as the membrane.

Under-sink RO runs far higher. A high-capacity under-sink model such as RP-U03 delivers 1000–1600 GPD, and a hydrogen-water under-sink model such as RP-U04 delivers 1000 GPD at 2.6 L/min. That is a different class of product: it fills a kettle in seconds, supplies a whole family's drinking and cooking water, and keeps up when several people use water at once.

Rated purified volume follows the same pattern. Our under-sink models are rated at 4000–8000 L, against 2000 L for a countertop RO. Both figures are national-standard grade minimums — actual volume is higher — but the gap tells you which form is built for heavy daily use.

If your market is small households, offices and rentals, countertop flow is enough. If your market is families who cook and drink from the same system all day, under-sink flow is what they will judge you on.

Hot, cold and ice: what countertop adds

Countertop is where the extra functions live. A countertop RO can add compressor cooling, instant heating, and even ice making in one unit. Our RP-C09 combines RO with ice, hot and cold water in a single countertop machine, and RP-C10 adds compressor cooling with hot and cold water. The buyer gets a drinks station, not just a filter.

Under-sink systems are more focused. They purify water at high flow and deliver it through a faucet — some with a heated faucet or a digital display — but they do not make ice and they do not chill a large volume. A hydrogen-water under-sink model such as RP-U04 adds dissolved hydrogen (≥2000 ppb) for buyers who want that function, but the form itself stays simple: purified water, on tap, at high flow.

So the choice is also a choice about what you are selling. Countertop sells convenience and features in one box. Under-sink sells capacity and a permanent installation.

Where each one sells

Countertop sells best where installation is a barrier: rented flats, small kitchens, offices, dormitories, and markets where buyers expect to take the machine home and use it the same day. It also sells as a second machine — for a bedroom, an office desk area, or a holiday home.

Under-sink sells best where the buyer owns the home and wants a permanent solution: family houses, new kitchens, and renovations. It is also the form that distributors sell with installation service, because the fitting is part of the product.

Most brands that grow beyond a single model end up carrying both: a countertop machine for the fast, no-installation sale, and an under-sink system for the household that wants high flow. The two forms do not compete with each other — they cover different buyers.

How to choose for your market

Start from the buyer's home, not the machine. If your buyers rent, move often, or have small kitchens, lead with countertop. If they own houses and cook for a family, lead with under-sink. If you are not sure, a countertop machine is the lower-risk first model: it needs no installation, so it sells to the widest pool, and the sample tells you quickly whether the market wants it.

FAQ

Which sells better, countertop or under-sink?
It depends on the market. Countertop sells to the widest pool because it needs no installation. Under-sink sells to homeowners and families who want high flow and a permanent system. Many brands carry both.

Does a countertop RO need a plumber?
No. Place it on the worktop, fill the raw-water tank and plug it in.

How much water can an under-sink RO deliver?
A high-capacity under-sink model delivers 1000–1600 GPD — for example, RP-U04 delivers 1000 GPD at 2.6 L/min. That fills a kettle in seconds and keeps up with a family's daily use.

Can one machine do hot, cold and ice?
A countertop machine can. RP-C09 combines RO with ice, hot and cold water in one unit. Under-sink systems focus on high-flow purified water rather than ice or chilling.
