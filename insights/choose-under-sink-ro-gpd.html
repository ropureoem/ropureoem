How to Choose an Under-Sink RO Purifier by GPD: 400G vs 600G vs 800G vs 1000G

27 November 2026

Short answer: for most family kitchens, 600–800 GPD is the right range. 400 GPD suits small households and apartments. 1000 GPD and above is for large households, or for buyers who want the fastest fill. GPD is a membrane rating over 24 hours, not the flow you get at the tap — so always check the published flow in litres per minute alongside the GPD number.

What GPD actually means

GPD stands for gallons per day. It is the membrane's rated production capacity over 24 hours under test conditions. A 400 GPD membrane can in theory produce 400 gallons of purified water in a day; a 1000 GPD membrane can produce 1000 gallons.

Two things buyers get wrong about this number:

  1. GPD is not tap flow. The flow you feel at the faucet is measured in litres per minute (L/min), and it depends on inlet pressure, water temperature and the system's design — not only on the membrane. Two 800 GPD models can have different published flows.
  2. A bigger number is not always better. A larger membrane costs more and needs more space. If the household never uses the extra capacity, the buyer paid for performance nobody notices.

Our earlier guide on choosing by flow rate makes the same point from the other direction: a countertop model such as RP-C09 is rated 50 / 75 / 100 GPD, while a high-capacity under-sink model such as RP-U03 is rated 1000 / 1200 / 1600 GPD. The right number follows the demand, not the other way round.

The four tiers, with real models

400 GPD — small households and apartments

400 GPD is the entry tier for under-sink RO. It suits one- or two-person households, apartments, and buyers who mainly need drinking and cooking water rather than a high-flow kitchen tap.

  • RP-U07 (M925R): 400–600 GPD, PPC + RO + C filtration, rated purified volume 4000 L. A straightforward mid-capacity unit with no heating and no extra features.
  • RP-U14 (AM607R): 400 / 600 / 800 GPD in one slim body (126 × 412 × 353 mm), RO / filtered flow of 1.04–2.1 / 2.5 L/min, rated purified volume 5000 L, noise ≤53 dB(A). Built for narrow under-sink spaces that still need a real capacity choice.

A 400 GPD tier is also available inside a built-in tank family (see “Tank vs tankless” below), where a small membrane plus a tank delivers high initial flow.

600 GPD — the family-kitchen standard

600 GPD is the most common choice for a family kitchen: enough for drinking, cooking and filling pots without waiting, without the cost and size of the top tier.

  • RP-U01 (M2): 600 / 800 / 1000 GPD on the main model, pure-water flow 2.6 L/min (initial 3 L/min), rated purified volume 4000 L, 386 × 146 × 334 mm.
  • RP-U11 (AM606R family): main option 600 / 800 / 1000 GPD, 315 × 203 × 427 mm, rated purified volume 6000 L, noise ≤53 dB(A), pure-to-drain ratio 2:1.
  • RP-U05 (X612): one compact platform covering 400 / 600 / 800 / 1000 / 1200 GPD, main model 400 × 130 × 320 mm, PPC + RO filtration, rated purified volume 6000 L, with dual-water, recirculation and heating options.

800 GPD — busy households

800 GPD suits larger families and kitchens where several people draw water at the same time, or where the purifier also feeds a hot-water function.

  • RP-U09 (M2102R), high-capacity tier: 800–1400 GPD, flow 2.5 L/min, 382 × 148 × 358.5 mm, rated purified volume 4000 L.

  • RP-U14 (AM607R) also reaches 800 GPD in the same slim 126 mm body, for buyers who want the higher tier without a wider cabinet.

1000 GPD and above — large households and fast fill

1000 GPD and above is for large households, offices and food-service use, and for buyers whose main complaint is “the water comes out too slowly.”

  • RP-U04 (W9): 1000 GPD, pure-water flow 2.6 L/min, PCC composite pre-filter + RO, rated purified volume 4000 L, 420 × 170 × 420 mm.

  • RP-U11-2 (M906R): fixed 1000 GPD, flow 2.6 L/min, rated purified volume 4500 L. The same family also offers a 1000 / 1200 GPD option (AM901R) with zero waste water.
  • RP-U05 (X612): the platform extends to 1200 GPD, and RP-U03 (per our flow-rate guide) is rated 1000 / 1200 / 1600 GPD.

Tank vs tankless: a second way to get high flow

GPD is not the only way to get fast water. The RP-U06 family pairs a small membrane with a built-in tank, so the tank — not the membrane — delivers the flow:

  • <strong>Dual-engine option:</strong> 400G membrane with a 2 L pressure tank, 1.05 L/min pure-water flow, and over 4.2 L/min in the first 3 seconds.
  • <strong>Other options:</strong> a 4.1 L tank (100G membrane, 4–9 L/min), an 8 L tank with UV (50–150G, 2.5–5 L/min), and a 7.5 L tank with static UV (75/100G, 5 L/min).
  • All four options have a rated purified volume of 4000 L.

For a buyer, the practical difference is this: a tankless 1000 GPD unit gives a steady high flow from a large membrane; a tank unit gives a strong initial flow from stored water, with a smaller, cheaper membrane behind it. Which one fits depends on whether the household draws water in short bursts or in long, continuous fills.

What GPD does not tell you

  • Inlet pressure and temperature. Published flows assume stated inlet conditions (for example, RP-U14 lists an inlet of 0.1–0.4 MPa and 5–38 °C). Low pressure or cold water reduces real-world flow.
  • How long the filters last. That is the rated purified volume — 4000 L, 5000 L or 6000 L depending on the model — not the GPD number. Our filter-life guide explains how that volume translates into replacement intervals.
  • <strong>Noise.</strong> A larger membrane does not have to be louder: RP-U11 and RP-U14 both run at ≤53 dB(A) across their tiers.
  • Certification. Capacity has nothing to do with certification. Which certificates a model carries depends on the model and the target market — see our Europe certification guide.

How to choose: a 30-second checklist

  1. <strong>Count the people.</strong> 1–2 people: 400 GPD is enough. 3–5 people: 600–800 GPD. 6 or more, or an office: 1000 GPD and above.
  2. <strong>Check the flow, not just the GPD.</strong> Compare the published L/min figures in the table above.
  3. <strong>Measure the cabinet.</strong> A slim 126 mm body (RP-U14) fits a narrow cabinet; a 420 mm cube (RP-U04) needs more room.
  4. <strong>Decide on hot water.</strong> If the household wants hot water from the same unit, the heated configurations (RP-U08) matter more than the GPD tier.
  5. <strong>Consider a tank</strong> if the household draws water in short bursts — a small membrane plus a tank can beat a bigger membrane on initial flow.

FAQ

Is 1000 GPD too much for a normal family?
Not harmful — the unit simply has spare capacity. But the buyer pays more for the larger membrane and needs a bigger cabinet. For a 3–4 person household, 600–800 GPD usually fills a glass or a pot fast enough.

Does a higher GPD mean cleaner water?
No. GPD is about how much water the membrane can produce, not how well it filters. Filtration quality comes from the filter stages (for example PPC + RO, or PCC composite + RO) and the rated purified volume.

Can one platform cover several GPD tiers?
Yes. Several of our under-sink families are built this way — RP-U05 (X612) covers 400 to 1200 GPD on one platform, and RP-U09 covers 400 to 1400 GPD across two tiers — so a brand can offer a whole capacity range without stocking unrelated machines.

What should I ask a supplier before ordering?
Ask for the published flow in L/min at a stated inlet pressure, the rated purified volume, the cabinet dimensions, and which certificates the exact model carries for your market. GPD alone is not a complete specification.
